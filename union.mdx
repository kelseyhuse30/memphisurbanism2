## Relevant Academic Studies

_Note that I haven't reviewed all of these personally, but was sent this list regarding research on where pedestrians will cross roads._

<Card title="Guo, H., Zhao, F., Wang, W., Zhou, Y., Zhang, Y., & Wets, G. (2014). **Modeling the perceptions and preferences of pedestrians on crossing facilities**. Discrete Dynamics in Nature and Society, 2014, Article 949475.[ https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/949475](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/949475)" href="https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/949475">
  Distance finding: The modeled compliance index declined particularly rapidly between 75 and 150 m of additional walking, indicating increasing preference for crossing outside designated facilities as the detour increased (§4.2, Figure 5). Wiley Online Library
</Card>

<Card title="Cantillo, V., Arellana, J., & Rolong, M. (2015). **Modelling pedestrian crossing behaviour in urban roads: A latent variable approach**. Transportation Research Part F: Traffic Psychology and Behaviour, 32, 56–67.[ https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trf.2015.04.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trf.2015.04.008)" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trf.2015.04.008">
  Distance finding: Longer walking distances to signalized crosswalks or pedestrian bridges increased the probability of crossing directly, with a stronger effect for bridges, but the verified abstract provides no numerical distance threshold. ScienceDirect
</Card>

<Card title="Xie, K., Yang, H., Ishak, S., Zhai, G., & Yan, Z. (2024). **Factors influencing pedestrian decisions to cross mid-block and potential countermeasures** (Report No. VTRC 25-R6). Virginia Transportation Research Council. Full report" href="https://vtrc.virginia.gov/reports/all-reports/25-r6/">
  Distance finding: In one modeled example, reducing distance to the nearest crosswalk by 100 or 200 ft reduced predicted midblock crossing probability from 71% to 64% or 58%, respectively; these are site-specific predictions rather than additional-route-distance thresholds (Table 11)
</Card>

<Card title="Balk, S. A., Bertola, M. A., Shurbutt, J., & Do, A. (2014). **Human factors assessment of pedestrian roadway crossing behavior** (Report No. FHWA-HRT-13-098). Federal Highway Administration. Full report" href="https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/13098/index.cfm">
  Distance finding: Distance between marked crosswalks contributed to predicting crossing location, but the study does not establish a universal acceptable additional walking distance. FHWA-HRT-13-098
</Card>

<Card title="Han, S., & Chang, J. S. (2021). **Identifying priority crosswalk locations in urban road networks**. Journal of Urban Planning and Development, 147(2), Article 04021014.[ https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)UP.1943-5444.0000679](https://doi.org/10.1061/\(ASCE\)UP.1943-5444.0000679)" href="https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)UP.1943-5444.0000679">
  Distance finding: The study prioritizes crosswalk locations using pedestrian route costs and network connectivity, rather than establishing a numerical detour distance at which pedestrians decide to cross directly. Seoul National University
</Card>

<Card title="Yu, C., Ma, W., Lo, H. K., & Yang, X. (2015). **Optimization of mid-block pedestrian crossing network with discrete demands.** Transportation Research Part B: Methodological, 73, 103–121.[ https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trb.2014.12.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trb.2014.12.005)" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trb.2014.12.005">
  Distance finding: The optimization model incorporates pedestrian detour distance and signal delay as crossing costs, but does not establish an empirically observed maximum acceptable detour. ScienceDirect
</Card>

<Card title="García-Ramírez, Y., Arrobo-Herrera, F., Cruz-Cortez, A., Fernández-Garrido, L., Flores, J., Lara-Bayas, W., Lema-Nacipucha, C., Mejía-Caldas, D., Navas-Coque, R., Torres-Bermeo, H., & Zambrano-Delgado, K. (2026). **Visual context and behavioral priming in pedestrian crossing decisions: Evidence from a stated preference experiment in Ecuadorian urban areas.** Smart Cities, 9(1), Article 19.[ https://doi.org/10.3390/smartcities9010019](https://doi.org/10.3390/smartcities9010019)" href="https://doi.org/10.3390/smartcities9010019">
  Distance finding: Additional walking distance had modest and often statistically nonsignificant effects on crossing choice, so this study supplies no defensible distance cutoff
</Card>

<Card title="Memphis Innovation Corridor - basically defunct now?" icon="book" horizontal href="https://memphisinnovationcorridor.com/news.html">
  "How will the project change Union Avenue?"

  The design of the Memphis Innovation Corridor project will introduce a center left turn lane on Union Avenue from Manassas Street to East Parkway and convert Union Avenue from a six lane road to a five lane road. This change is expected to improve the overall flow of traffic and improve safety in the corridor.
</Card>

  "The Safe Systems Approach (SSA) aims to promote a culture of safety with the expectation that all users of the roadway system, regardless of mode, will be protected and that responsibility is shared with those who plan, build, maintain, and use the transportation system."

  The goal of the 2025-2029 Tennessee SHSP is to reduce fatalities and serious injuries below the historic trend by taking a proactive approach towards zero (0) deaths.
</Card>
