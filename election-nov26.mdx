# When to Vote

Election day is November 3, 2026. If you vote on election day, you can **only** vote at your designated polling location. You can find this location on your voter ID card, or by looking up your information on the Tennessee Registration Lookup website [here](https://tnmap.tn.gov/voterlookup/).

Early voting is Oct 14 - Oct 28. All early voting locations and hours are available [here](https://tnmap.tn.gov/voterlookup/earlyvoting/Shelby).

_(I hope to make a map of these locations and publish it here, so it's easier to locate an ideal early voting location.)_

<Tip>
  I **highly** recommend making a plan to vote early. You have more choice in a polling site, and lines are generally shorter.

  You also never know what emergencies or other events that might come up for you on voting day. 
</Tip>

## Memphis City Charter Amendments

There are 3 amendments proposed to the city charter on the November ballot. 

### Ordinance 5966

<Card title='Removes Public Library **employees** as "exempt" from Civil Service (Only change is in section i)' icon="newspaper" horizontal>
  Shall Article 34, Sec. 250 of the Home Rule Charter of the City of Memphis be amended to read as follows:

  'ARTICLE 34: CIVIL SERVICE

  The Director of Personnel shall classify all offices and positions in the City service according to the duties and responsibilities of each position, provided, however, that the following officers and employees shall be exempted from the provisions of this article:

  a. Officers and judges who are elected by popular vote and their successors;

  b. Members of and employees of the Board of Education;

  c. Members of the board and employees of the Memphis Light, Gas and Water Division;

  d. Division directors who are either now or may be hereafter appointed by the Mayor and Council;

  e. All employees of the legal department;

  f. Members of the various boards and commissions now existing or hereafter created;

  g. All staff employees of the offices of the Mayor and Chief Administrative Officer;

  h. One executive secretary for each board, commission, division director, and for the administrative judge of the City court;

  i. All officers of the Memphis and Shelby County Public Library;

  j. All employees of the City Council office;

  k. Such other officers or employees whose positions, in the judgment of the Mayor, with the concurrence of the City Council, cannot be subject to the rules herein provided, and who shall not be affected by such rules and regulations. The provisions of this subsection (k) shall not allow removal of civil service protection from any employee whose position at that time is covered by this article.'
</Card>

<Check>
  My recommendation: FOR

  Library employees should be part of the civil service, like other city employees. Their current appointed status allows for them to be hired or fired at any time for any reason, and that's not good! They want the protections civil service provides.
</Check>

This is the Library Workers Amendment to update their status from appointed to civil service.

When the public library workers were brought on as official city workers in the 80s, they were exempted from civil service. That's in our city charter today, and this amendment would take that out.

Civil service is the workforce that operates under the city and has strict guidelines for hiring, firing, etc. Most city employees are in the civil service including the parks department workers, sanitation workers, etc.

Since they are exempt from civil service (in Article 34, Section 9), that means that they are appointed by the mayor. Most library workers go through a standard hiring process, but this can be bypassed, and that's not good! Additionally, library workers don't feel like they can bring up their concerns or suggest improvements, because they can also be fired at any time -- that's also not good!

This amendment is a result of grassroots organizing from the [Memphis Library Supporters](https://www.instagram.com/memphislibrarysupporters/), and sponsored by Councilmembers Smiley, Canale, Cooper-Sutton, Easter-Thomas, Ford, Green, Spinosa, Swearengen-Washington, Walker, and Warren. (All of them except Carlisle, Logan, and White).

<Info>
  The only councilmember to vote against putting this amendment on the ballot was **Carlisle**, who said "I don't think civil service is a mechanism for the future. I think it is archaic."

  He mentions "rolling" people for 36+ months without any type of employment (I assume during an employment termination appeal) and says the process has to be fixed.

  Seems reasonable, but he does not present any real data, background, or specifics regarding how to fix this issue. I think we deserve more explanation here.
</Info>

### Ordinance 5977

<Card title="Lobbyist Registration" icon="newspaper" horizontal>
  Shall the Home Rule Charter of the City of Memphis, Tennessee, be amended to require the Memphis City Council, by ordinance, to create a lobbyist registration and disclosure process to ensure transparency and accountability?
</Card>

<Card title="My recommendation: AGAINST, unless more information is released." type="danger" horizontal>
  We deserve more information and justification from the sponsor of this amendment (Carlisle).
</Card>

This amendment was proposed by Councilman Carlisle.

In 2020, he proposed lobbyist registration as a city ordinance, and that vote failed in the council at that time. Interestingly, councilmembers on our current council who voted against it then were Michalyn Easter-Thomas and Rhonda Logan. The councilmembers who voted for it (who are still on council today) were J. Ford Canale, Chase Carlisle, Edmund Ford, Sr., and Jeff Warren. J.B. Smiley, Jr. abstained.

So here we are in 2026, and the council unanimously decided to put this question to the Memphis voters.

What did council have to say about it? Hardly anything.

It first appeared in a committee agenda on June 6, 2026. Unfortunately that day's committee meetings do not appear to be archived on the city's YouTube channel.

Then it was on the consent agenda (not discussed, approved by all of council unanimously) for the first reading on June 23, and for the second reading on July 7. For the third and final reading on August 4, it passed unanimously and finally we hear from the sponsor, Councilman Carlisle: 

> "I think that governments, especially large city governments all over the country are starting to look at lobbyist registry and all sorts of transparency opportunities. And I think for us, this is just low-hanging fruit and something, you know, I think should have been done a long time ago. But it's really not about what I think. We're just asking the voters to say 'Hey, we want the council to form this.' And so I ask for your support to put it on the ballot." - Councilman Carlisle

Then only one councilmember responded:

> “We really need to explain to the constituents what this is. We don’t want them to be ignorant to it”. - Councilwoman Cooper-Sutton

That explanation? We haven't received it.

With so little public discussion about what this really means, it appears that this was deal done behind the public's back.

Why did this come up? Originally, it had to do with the possibility of MLGW finding other power providers other than TVA, which could save costs for consumers. This is something I haven't had a lot of time to look into, but have pasted some sources into below.

Here's the thing: a lot of amendments seem good on paper. "Lobbyist registry" -- who would argue with that? But our council members should educate the public about WHY it's necessary to change the city charter to make this happen.

I think Councilwoman Cooper-Sutton was right,<u> constituents deserve explanations</u>.
