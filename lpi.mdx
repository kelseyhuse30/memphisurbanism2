# What is it?

A leading pedestrian interval (LPI) gives people walking a **head start** across the street. The walk sign comes on 3-7 seconds **before** the green light for cars. Walkers can be seen in the crosswalk before a driver tries to turn left or right. Drivers have to wait just a few more seconds!

# Benefits

- People walking are **seen** by drivers
- Drivers are more likely to **slow down** and **stop** to let people walk across the street
- **Safer** for walkers who need more **time** to cross
- Crashes go **down** by **13%** when a crossing is changed to give pedestrians a head start

# Backstory

When I lived in Austin, I frequently crossed the large state-owned highway that I lived on (South Lamar). One crossing in particular was extremely sketchy, because the walk sign came on at the same time as the left turning light for the cars. A friend suggested that I log a 311 request to get a "leading pedestrian interval" (LPI) at this location, and I loved the result! I made a [TikTok video]([ttps://www.tiktok.com/@buttercreamgang/video/7215025178349178154]\(https://www.tiktok.com/@buttercreamgang/video/7215025178349178154\)) documenting it.

Since I learned about LPIs, I am an advocate for them everywhere. They can often be implemented without any new infrastructure, making them essentially free (it only requires a technician to change the light timing).

Sometimes, an intersection is too old to implement an LPI. If there are no "walk/don't walk" pedestrian signs, the intersection will not be viable for this change. They do **not** require a pedestrian beg button to be implemented, though.

# How to Request One

The intersection **must** have traffic lights with pedestrian signals (walk/don't walk) in order to implement an LPI.

Email the street crossing to [**engineering@memphistn.gov**](mailto:engineering@memphistn.gov) and tell them your story and request!

# Memphis Locations

# Case Studies

- **Austin**: In the summer of 2024, Austin proactively installed over 640 LPIs throughout the city to increase safety for pedestrians. ([Source ](https://mailchi.mp/austintexas/austin-mobility-news-08-05-24)-- Austin Mobility newsletter)
- **Nashville**: Nashville is currently working on implementing LPIs at 336 intersections across their city. ([Source ](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/transportation/traffic-management-center/signal-retiming-project)-- Nashville govt)
